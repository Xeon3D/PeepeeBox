* The NSM DATAprint 3000 joins the DPU-414 as a selectable printer, rebuilt from the documented hardware: the real lamp order and paper direction, the case-edge controls and three-button setup keyboard, the SRAM card, and the print, delete, initialise and recovery workflows an operator actually runs. Paper-out, transfer errors, write protection, timeouts and a faulted card all behave as they do on the machine, and a report is never committed when the card is checksum-invalid, reset or pulled. Pick the model in **Tools > Printer type**; the choice is remembered.
* The DATAprint's Epson M-160 has its own sound: solenoid impact chatter, the lead-cam motor, ribbon and feed mechanics, tearing the 57 mm paper, and the documented beeper cadences. **DATAprint 3000 in English** translates the legends, the card label and every firmware message for anyone who does not read the German original.
* Both printer windows now follow Windows display scaling, with the hit targets where they look, and each follows its real paper path -- the DPU-414's roll grows upward above its controls, the DATAprint's receipt grows downward below them.
* The nightly modem call sounds like one. The cabinets set `ATM1L3`, speaker on until carrier and loud, so the call was always audible: dial tone, the number in DTMF, ringback, then the V.8 answer tone and V.34 or V.90 training, in UK or CEPT tones according to the country the cabinet set. The call also takes as long as a real one whether or not you hear it -- dialling paces itself digit by digit, the far end answers after a ring, training runs its full length before CONNECT, and an unanswered call rings on before NO CARRIER. **Tools > Sound > Modem sounds** turns the speaker off; it is on by default.
* The toolbar's dongle button opens a dialog that puts the dongle's own settings first -- identity, Version, Territory and the parallel HASP -- built from the device's config table, so a setting added there turns up here too. The machine licence moves behind **Machine licence...**, which shows the licence in force and the serial it becomes, with "Random from the pool" and "Default". Nothing is written until OK, so Cancel now undoes the licence too; the old hand-off to Options saved on its own and could not be taken back.
